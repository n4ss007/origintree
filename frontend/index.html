How a lineage is traced

Every branch is a record, not a guess.

NCBI Taxonomy holds a classification for roughly two million named organisms, each one placed under a parent it descended from in the scheme — species under genus, genus under family, on up through kingdom and domain. OriginTree walks that chain of parents from your organism back to the root of the record and draws it as the branch you see above: not an illustration of evolution, but a citation trail through it.

Some names are shared by organisms with no relation to one another at all — a search for "panda" alone resolves to a bear, a red panda, a catfish, and more. When that happens, OriginTree says so plainly and asks which one you meant, rather than guessing on your behalf.

Every taxonomic fact shown is read from NCBI, and every DNA base from a real GenBank record — nothing on this page is generated. OriginTree is a research and educational project, not a clinical or diagnostic tool. It stores no personal data and sets no cookies; searches are sent to NCBI and GBIF to answer them.
